THE PREPAREDNESS BASELINE

Why start with 72 hours?

72 hours is a practical starting point for household preparation. Use it to build a plan around your household’s needs, and follow current official advice.

72
HOURS
01 IRELAND’S ADVICE

Start with essentials for your household.

Ireland’s Be Emergency Ready booklet advises keeping a 72-hour supply of non-perishable food and recommends 2–3 litres of drinking water per person per day. It also points households to medicines, lighting, warmth and individual needs.

Irish Government · Be Emergency Ready
02 EU GUIDANCE

The first days can put response systems under pressure.

The European Commission’s 2026 population-preparedness guidance sets at least 72 hours as a self-sufficiency baseline. It says response systems may be under significant pressure in this initial period while logistics and assistance scale up.

Read why the EU uses 72 hours ↗
03 WHY IT HELPS

Basic readiness can leave urgent help for those who need it most.

Crises with different causes can disrupt the same essentials: electricity, water, communications or supplies. Being able to cover basic needs for a short initial period may ease pressure on responders and help assistance reach people in greater need sooner.

EU guidance · preparedness rationale, not a duration statistic
i

Make a plan that fits your household. Start with the essentials you would need for three days, including water, food, medicines, warmth and information. Adapt for your location and household needs, and follow current official instructions, including evacuation advice.

WHY READY72 EXISTS

Preparedness is about
understanding the knock-on effects.

A storm can affect power, water, transport and communications. A cyber advisory can matter only if it applies to technology you use. READY72 brings the evidence and practical guidance together without claiming more than the sources show.
