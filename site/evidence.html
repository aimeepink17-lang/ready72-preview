READY72 BIG-DATA ANALYSIS · 2016–2025

Does heavy rain connect to recorded flooding?

The ten-year Met Éireann grid analysis is a strong starting dataset, but rainfall alone cannot show whether floods are becoming more frequent. Its annual heavy-rain measure rises and falls: 1.72% of valid grid estimates in 2016, 3.27% in 2023 and 3.06% in 2025 at the 20 mm/day comparison level.

The stronger question is whether heavy rainfall in a place and its surrounding catchment is associated with reported flooding or unusually high river levels in the following days. That means joining rainfall to dated, located OPW observations and flood records.

WHY PREPAREDNESS MATTERS NOW

More digital links mean more ways disruption can travel.

Cyber risk is not a prediction that Ireland’s power grid will be attacked. It is a reason to prepare for interruptions to services people depend on.

IRELAND · NCSC 2025 NATIONAL CYBER RISK ASSESSMENT

Essential services are connected—and the risk can cascade.

Ireland’s NCSC says deeper digital reliance is making the threat landscape more complex and increasing incident risk. It identifies potential cross-sector impacts, including energy, transport, healthcare and finance, and calls for resilience across society.

DOCUMENTED ENERGY-SECTOR INCIDENT · POLAND · 29 DECEMBER 2025

Energy systems have been targeted. Defences prevented a wider outage.

Polish authorities reported coordinated cyberattacks against renewable-energy operators and combined heat and power plants. The national electricity supply was not disrupted. This shows a credible pathway and the value of operational defences; it is not evidence of an attack on Ireland, and the report does not attribute this incident to AI.

What the evidence supports: take practical, all-hazards preparedness seriously because connected services can be disrupted and AI is changing the tools available to attackers. What it does not support: claiming an attack on Ireland’s electricity grid is imminent, or that AI alone explains an increase in attacks. WHAT THE EVIDENCE CAN — AND CANNOT — SAY

Past change is a reason to prepare, not a promise of what comes next.

Use observations to understand what has happened, and climate models to explore plausible future conditions. Neither can tell us exactly when, where or how long a disruption will happen.

01 OBSERVED IN IRELAND

Heavy rain is changing in some places.

EPA analysis finds increasing intensity of heavy rainfall, notably in eastern and south-eastern Ireland. It also finds no persistent trend in annual rainfall totals or consecutive wet days. The evidence is mixed by measure and location.

02 MODELLED SCENARIOS · AROUND 2050

Models suggest heavier multi-day rainfall is possible.

For Ireland, projected change in maximum five-day rainfall is about +7% under two emissions scenarios, compared with 1976–2005. The model ranges are wide and include both decreases and increases.

So why prepare?

EPA identifies extreme wind damage and disruption to energy and communications as urgent climate risks. Storms can affect connected services, as seen in recent Irish events. The data supports being ready for a range of plausible disruptions, because a small household plan can reduce immediate strain while services are restored. It does not say a specific event is imminent or guarantee an outcome.
